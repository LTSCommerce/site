Are Agency Footer Links Really Ethical?

Created on 2019-11-14 09:49

Published on 2019-11-14 10:35

As part of my role at Edmonds Commerce I get to look at a lot of websites, I mean, really quite a lot.

One thing that I see that always troubles me is when e-commerce websites have a site wide footer link that has clearly been put their by their agency. Some poor souls even have multiple links in there that are leaking huge amounts of SEO benefits to the people they are paying I'd assume good money. It's a breach of trust.

Examples

I decided not to name names, so I'm not going to post any screen shots. But if you see a website and in the footer is something along the lines of:

PPC and e-commerce web design by Agency

displayed on every page footer, that's what I'm referring to. If you have this kind of thing on your own e-commerce site, well you might want to get rid of it!

Less abusive but still siphoning are footer links like:

Site by Agency Name

If you want to find examples, search for anything really and go down to page 10 of the results and see what I mean.

First a very quick primer:

Google made a very interesting observation when trying to figure out how to rank the huge number of pages in their index when people search for something. What they realised is that the higher quality pages tend to have more links from other pages pointing to them. They called this "Page Rank" This simple metric become the bedrock of the algorithm that Google and other search engines use to determine which websites to display first in their rankings.

Whilst these days things are a lot more complex, links still play a huge role in ranking.

Basically, the more links you have pointing to your site, the better you rank in the search engines.

Another key point, especially historically, was the text of the links pointing to your site. If the link text often had the acronym "PPC" in there, then you might have a better chance for ranking for PPC related searches.

SEOs often talk about the metaphor of "link juice" which is a pretty reasonable explanation of how an algorithm might calculate and apportion Page Rank. Link juice is received from inbound links from other pages and is shared amongst the outbound links evenly.

This means that if a page contains links back to the agency, that agency is potentially siphoning off valuable link juice from their own clients!

Link building

So a large part of SEO campaigns has always been a campaign to build links from as many high quality sites as possible, ideally with a certain percentage with valuable key phrases in there.

This process can be very expensive and many companies will even pay large sums of money to pay for link placement on websites.

Links have an undeniable value and it can have a clear monetary value.

So why would anyone want to give their agency a site wide link (as in every single page of their website) that siphons off SEO link juice back to the agency?

My Opinion - It is Entirely Unethical

It could not be clearer to me that this is an underhanded and exploitative tactic by agencies to take advantage of the lack of technical understanding on the part of their clients in order to feather their own nests in terms of boosting the agency's SEO position, potentially at the detriment of the actual client site.

I thought about naming and shaming, and decided against it.

You never see it on big websites, it's always the SME level sites who probably don't have the in-house expertise to know that this is not something they should accept.

Furthermore, It is Behind The Times

By placing these kinds of backlinks, the agency is actually demonstrating a lack of understanding of current SEO trends. There is a strong chance that an over optimised site wide footer link may actually penalise the site it is pointed at. So not only are the agency being unethical, but they are not even gaining much benefit and potentially running the risk of being penalised.

The Exception - "nofollow" or "sponsored"

One exception where there should not be any detrimental SEO issues is if the agency links have a "nofollow" or "sponsored" attribute in the link tag. This means that whilst it it s a real link, the search engines are instructed not to follow the link nor to attribute any link juice to it.

You can see this by right clicking the link and selecting "inspect element" which should show you the HTML for the link in question.
